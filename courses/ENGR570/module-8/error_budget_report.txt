Step 1 - baseline
==============================================================================
  Module 6/7 estimator: 1920 x 1080 camera, 20 deg HFOV, 20 stars, 0.2 px; accelerometer 0.005 m/s^2
  noise and 0.005 m/s^2 bias; misalignment 0.05 deg; time 0.1 s; no atmospheric correction; WGS84-normal
  vertical; Module 2 Earth orientation. 10 observations per instrument, boresight 30 deg from zenith.
  New in this module, as assumptions about the real world: standard atmosphere, stellar aberration and
  proper motion, deflection of the vertical 5 arcsec per component, principal point 0.5 px, focal
  length 0.05 %, lens distortion k1 = 0.02. All illustrative; none is a verified specification.

  Same estimator in the idealized Module 6/7 world: RMS 9.05 km, 95th percentile 16.55 km.
  In the physically perturbed world:                 RMS 9.20 km, 95th percentile 15.60 km.

  The sensitivities of independent sources are their shares of the error variance, so they sum to
  about one. In the baseline the leverage is all in alignment and bias. Once those are dealt with
  it moves to whatever is then largest. Sensitivity shows where accuracy leverage is, not which
  improvement is affordable.

Step 5 - predicted uncertainty versus Monte Carlo
==============================================================================
  scenario                                      actual RMS  reported RMS  mean NEES  95% coverage
  Baseline, fixed errors not declared              9.19 km         129 m    11617.0          0.0%
  Baseline, uncertainties declared                 9.20 km       9.15 km        2.0         95.2%
  Improved, uncertainties declared                    92 m          92 m        2.0         94.6%
  Declared = accelerometer bias and calibration as shared errors (Module 7),
  plus clock and deflection added to the position covariance. NOT in any
  covariance: the biases from unmodelled refraction and aberration, and
  focal-length or distortion errors.
  In the baseline those undeclared biases (about 1 km) hide inside a 9 km
  declared uncertainty, so coverage looks healthy for the wrong reason.
  Clean residuals and a plausible ellipse do not show that the model is
  complete; they show that the remaining errors are small beside the ones
  that were declared.

Against the two objectives
==============================================================================
  Baseline   median 7.85 km, RMS 9.20 km, 95th 15.60 km;  within 10 NM 98%, 6 NM 79%, 200 m 0%, 100 m 0%
  Improved   median 75 m, RMS 92 m, 95th 161 m;  within 10 NM 100%, 6 NM 100%, 200 m 99%, 100 m 70%

  Dominant limitations, baseline: camera-IMU misalignment and accelerometer
  bias, which are fixed instrument errors that no amount of repeated
  observation removes.
  Dominant limitations, improved: accelerometer noise (72 m), deflection of the vertical (41 m), accelerometer bias (16 m).
  Remaining uncertainties this model does not cover: real star density and
  identification errors, real atmospheric conditions and their errors,
  measured deflection data, unmodelled acceleration (Module 5), thermal
  drift of the calibration, and whether the assumed error sizes are right.
